NTE is visually ambitious, so the useful question is not simply whether your PC can open it. You need enough graphics performance, storage headroom, and frame-rate stability for exploration and combat to feel consistent.

## NTE official PC requirements

The official launch FAQ lists an Intel Core i7-10700 with an NVIDIA GeForce GTX 1660 or AMD Radeon RX 5600 as the minimum PC level. The recommended level is an Intel Core i7-12700 with an RTX 3060 or RX 6700.

Windows 10 64-bit or newer is required. The FAQ asks for at least 60 GB of free storage and warns that installation or extraction may temporarily need another 60 GB. Leave comfortable headroom instead of filling the drive to its limit.

| Target | CPU | GPU |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Minimum | Intel Core i7-10700 or equivalent | GTX 1660 / RX 5600 or equivalent |
| Recommended | Intel Core i7-12700 or equivalent | RTX 3060 / RX 6700 or equivalent |

## Best settings order for a smoother first session

Start with the game’s recommended preset, then change one setting at a time. Lower shadows, volumetric effects, reflections, and crowd density before reducing texture quality. Textures mostly depend on available video memory, while shadows and effects can create heavier moment-to-moment load.

Use a frame-rate cap your PC can hold consistently. A stable 60 FPS usually feels better than an unlocked rate that repeatedly jumps and drops. If the game supports an upscaler, begin with its quality mode and compare motion clarity before choosing a more aggressive performance mode.

## Storage and driver checklist

Install NTE on an SSD, update the graphics driver, close unnecessary overlays, and keep extra storage available for patches. After a major update, let shader compilation finish before judging performance.

## What to test

Test a quiet street, a busy city area, fast travel, and combat. Watch for frame-time spikes, texture loading, input response, and fan noise. Change only one option between tests so you know what actually helped.

## Gamerzeta recommendation

A system near the recommended specification is the safer target for a visually rich open-world experience. On lower-end hardware, prioritize stable frame pacing and readable image quality over the highest preset.
